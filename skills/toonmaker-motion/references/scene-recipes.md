# Scene Recipes for UI-Led Motion Graphics

Use these as compositional patterns, not rigid templates. Choose the pattern that helps the viewer understand the idea.

## Opening hook

- Place one sharp question or surprising but verified statement in a large headline panel.
- Support it with one visual clue, icon, number, or compact illustration.
- Reveal the context after the hook has registered.
- Avoid long animated logos or an unrelated character introduction.

## Step-by-step process

- Use numbered cards, a progress line, or a sequence of connected panels.
- Highlight one stage at a time while keeping the overall sequence visible.
- Move the active step forward along a clear direction.
- Use labels that name the action, not vague labels such as “Step One” alone.

## Before-and-after comparison

- Use two aligned panels with consistent scale and matching labels.
- Reveal the changed property directly: move, resize, recolor, or replace the relevant part.
- Keep both states visible long enough to compare.
- Do not use color alone to distinguish the two sides.

## Cause and effect

- Place the cause and result in separate nodes or cards.
- Animate a connector, flow line, or state change between them.
- Introduce intermediate steps when the relationship is not immediate.
- Avoid implying causation when the source only supports correlation.

## Data point or statistic

- Make the number readable and give it a plain-language label.
- Use a chart only when the underlying values and scale are available.
- Animate the chart from a meaningful baseline and preserve axis integrity.
- Include the source or date when the statistic may change over time.

## Abstract concept

- Translate the concept into a spatial metaphor such as layers, connections, containers, paths, or balance.
- Label the metaphor so the audience knows what it represents.
- Do not let the metaphor introduce a false scientific or technical mechanism.

## Definition

- Put the term in a clear title card.
- Follow with a short plain-language definition.
- Show one concrete example and, when helpful, one non-example.
- Avoid decorative motion that distracts from the wording.

## Summary

- Bring together the main cards, labels, or diagram nodes from earlier scenes.
- Reduce the composition to the few ideas the viewer should remember.
- End on a clear final statement and allow it to hold before the video ends.

## Transition principles

- Prefer spatially motivated transitions: cards align, panels expand, lines connect, elements move into a new diagram, or the camera-like viewport shifts within a fixed graphic stage.
- Preserve an item's position or identity when it reappears so the viewer can follow it.
- Use a clean cut when the next scene is conceptually separate.
- Avoid transitions that are longer or louder than the information they connect.
