# Research and Fact-Checking for Explainer Scripts

Use this reference when the topic includes factual, scientific, historical, legal, medical, financial, technical, or current information.

## Establish the source basis

Prioritize sources in this order where applicable:

1. User-supplied documents or data when the user says they are the source of truth.
2. Primary sources: official datasets, original research, standards, laws, documentation, and direct institutional publications.
3. High-quality secondary sources that explain or review primary evidence.
4. Reputable reporting for recent developments.

Do not treat search snippets, unsourced social posts, or a generated summary as primary evidence.

## Build a claim list

Before scripting, identify claims that materially affect the explanation. For each claim, record:

- Exact claim in plain language.
- Source and publication date where available.
- Whether the evidence directly supports it.
- Any important qualification or uncertainty.
- Whether it is stable background knowledge or time-sensitive.

Do not over-research simple creative topics. Research only where accuracy materially matters.

## Script safely and accurately

- Keep the strength of the wording proportional to the evidence.
- Distinguish fact, interpretation, analogy, hypothetical example, and opinion.
- Do not invent figures, quotations, case studies, study results, or expert agreement.
- Do not simplify a mechanism so far that it becomes false.
- Preserve uncertainty when evidence is mixed or limited.
- Do not turn correlation into causation.
- For medical, legal, or financial subjects, avoid personalized professional advice and use suitable caveats.
- If sources conflict, disclose the disagreement instead of silently selecting the most convenient version.

## Use of supplied material

When the user supplies a PDF, report, spreadsheet, or article as the basis, inspect the relevant source itself. Preserve its terminology, framing, and findings. Do not fill gaps with invented values or replace its conclusion with generic outside knowledge. Clearly label any outside verification as separate from the supplied material.

## Source notes in the final script

List the sources used and associate them with the claims they support. Prefer direct links and publication dates when available. If no external factual source was needed, say so. Never create a decorative bibliography from sources that were not consulted.
